--- title: What is scaffold hopping? topic: lead-optimization order: 2 term: Scaffold hopping summary: Scaffold hopping replaces the core of an active molecule with a different chemical framework that keeps the key interactions, to gain new IP, better properties or easier synthesis. objectives: - Explain the goals of scaffold hopping - Describe the main types of scaffold hop - Know which computational methods support it ---

The scaffold of a molecule is its core framework — the ring systems and linkers that hold the functional groups in place. Scaffold hopping means replacing that core with a different one while keeping the groups that interact with the target in roughly the same 3D positions.

Why hop?

  • Intellectual property. A new core can escape a crowded patent space.
  • Properties. A different scaffold may fix poor solubility, metabolic instability or toxicity linked to the original core. See [pyxis_learn_link slug="what-is-admet"]ADMET[/pyxis_learn_link].
  • Synthesis. A more accessible core can make analogues cheaper and faster to produce.
  • Rescue. When a series stalls, a hop opens new structure–activity relationships.

Types of scaffold hop

  1. Heterocycle replacement — swapping atoms in a ring, for example a phenyl for a pyridine.
  2. Ring opening or closure — adding or removing a ring to change flexibility. Closing a large ring is the route to [pyxis_learn_link slug="what-is-a-macrocycle"]macrocycles[/pyxis_learn_link].
  3. Peptide mimicry — replacing a peptide backbone with a non-peptide scaffold that presents the same side chains.
  4. Topology-based hops — entirely new frameworks that share only the 3D arrangement of key features.

Computational support

Because a successful hop by definition looks different in 2D, standard [pyxis_learn_link slug="what-is-tanimoto-similarity"]2D fingerprint similarity[/pyxis_learn_link] is a weak guide. Useful methods include pharmacophore and 3D shape similarity, [pyxis_learn_link slug="what-is-molecular-docking"]docking[/pyxis_learn_link] of candidate replacements, and searches over libraries of 3D linkers or fragments that can bridge the groups you want to keep. The last approach is closely related to [pyxis_learn_link slug="what-is-fragment-linking"]fragment linking[/pyxis_learn_link].

Pyxis Discovery's Scaffolding Hop tool takes two 3D fragments, with chosen attachment atoms, and searches a library of 3D linkers for geometric matches that connect them while preserving their positions.
