DiffDock is a docking method introduced by researchers at MIT in 2022. Instead of searching poses and scoring them with a hand-built function, it generates poses with a diffusion model, the same family of generative AI used for image generation.

How it works

A diffusion model learns to reverse a noising process. During training, the ligand's correct pose from experimental structures is progressively scrambled — its position, orientation and torsion angles are randomised — and the network learns how to undo each step. At prediction time DiffDock starts from random poses and iteratively "denoises" them towards plausible binding modes on the protein.

A separate confidence model then ranks the generated poses by how likely they are to be close to correct.

DiffDock compared with classical docking

Classical (e.g. AutoDock Vina)DiffDock
Binding siteUsually a user-defined boxCan dock "blind" across the whole protein

Output scoreEstimated interaction energy (kcal/mol)Confidence that the pose is right
StrengthsWell understood; physically interpretableFast blind pose prediction; no box needed
Watch out forBox placement, scoring biasPhysically implausible poses; behaviour on targets unlike its training data

Using it well

  • Treat the confidence as a pose-quality signal, not as binding affinity. To rank compounds, combine it with a physics-based [pyxis_learn_link slug="what-is-a-docking-score"]score[/pyxis_learn_link] or experimental data.
  • Check poses for clashes and bad geometry; some versions of learned docking models can produce poses that look reasonable globally but are chemically strained.
  • Blind docking is useful when the [pyxis_learn_link slug="what-is-a-binding-site"]binding site[/pyxis_learn_link] is unknown. When it is known, confirm that top poses land there.

Because DiffDock is open source, it can be run on your own infrastructure, which matters when compound structures are confidential.
